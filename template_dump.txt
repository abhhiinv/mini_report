29: Abstract
30: Handwritten Text Recognition (HTR) has become an important research area in computer vision and artificial intelligence due to the large amount of handwritten information that still exists in educational, administrative, and historical documents. Converting handwritten text into machine-readable digital text enables efficient storage, searching, analysis, and automation of many tasks. However, recognizing handwritten text is challenging because handwriting styles vary significantly from person to person, characters may overlap, and writing may be inconsistent in size, spacing, and orientation. Traditional Optical Character Recognition (OCR) systems often struggle with these variations, making accurate handwritten text recognition a complex problem.
31: To address these challenges, this project develops an end-to-end Handwritten Text Recognition system built on a Convolutional Recurrent Neural Network (CRNN) architecture. The core recognition component uses a modified ResNet34 convolutional backbone for visual feature extraction, combined with a two-layer Bidirectional LSTM for sequence modeling, and a Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) decoder that aligns predicted character sequences with the input without requiring explicit character segmentation. The model is trained on the IAM Handwriting Database, a widely used benchmark dataset of word-level handwritten English images.
32: The system is deployed as a Django-based web application with an OpenCV preprocessing pipeline that segments full handwritten document images into individual word crops before recognition. The deployed system achieves approximately 87% character accuracy and 72% word accuracy on the IAM validation set, demonstrating its capability to digitize handwritten
33: documents effectively. The application provides an intuitive drag-and-drop interface for image upload, returns per-word transcriptions with confidence scores, and operates at sub-20 ms per-word inference latency on CPU hardware.

90: Introduction
91: Handwritten Text Recognition (HTR) has emerged as a critical and highly active research area within the fields of computer vision and artificial intelligence. This prominence is driven by the staggering volume of handwritten information that continues to persist across various sectors, including educational institutions, government administrations, and historical archives. While the world has moved toward a "digital-first" approach, billions of pages of legacy documents—ranging from student answer scripts and medical records to centuries-old manuscripts—remain locked in a non-searchable, physical format. Converting this handwritten text into machine-readable digital format is not merely a convenience; it is a necessity that enables efficient storage, rapid keyword searching, complex data analysis, and the large-scale automation of administrative tasks.
92: However, the transition from physical handwriting to digital text is fraught with technical difficulties that have challenged researchers for decades. Unlike printed text, which follows rigid fonts and standardized spacing, handwriting is inherently personal and infinitely variable. Recognizing handwritten text remains a significant challenge because handwriting styles vary extensively between individuals, influenced by factors such as culture, education, and even the writing instrument used. In many cases, characters in cursive script overlap or are connected by complex ligatures, making individual character isolation nearly impossible. Furthermore, real-world handwriting is often inconsistent in size, spacing, and orientation, frequently accompanied by noise such as ink bleeds, paper textures, or background artifacts. Traditional rule-based systems and early character-recognition technologies often fail to address these nuances, leading to high error rates and limited practical utility.
93: To overcome these formidable challenges, modern deep learning techniques have been widely adopted, shifting the paradigm from manual feature engineering to automated, data-driven learning. This project focuses on developing a robust and highly accurate Handwritten Text Recognition system designed to bridge the gap between human handwriting and digital intelligence. The core of this system is powered by a specialized hybrid neural network architecture known as the Convolutional Recurrent Neural Network (CRNN). This architecture is specifically chosen because it effectively marries the spatial feature extraction capabilities of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) with the sequential modeling power of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), creating a pipeline that understands both what a character looks like and where it fits within a sequenc0e.
